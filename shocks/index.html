External Shocks

Hypothetical sudden disruptions, not forecasts.

Strategic outlook published: 13 November 2026 · Manually curated and fixed until the next uploaded edition. Automated Radar scans do not change these assessments.

All 15 shocks below are hypothetical. They describe a possible sudden trigger, the route through which harm could spread, and what could limit it. The cited sources establish current relevant capabilities or pressures; they do not predict a shock.

  1. What if emergency spending cuts interrupt a European drone programme?

    Possible trigger: A sudden fiscal squeeze could move funding from experimental projects toward purchases needed immediately.

    How disruption could spread: Industrial partners would stop tests, teams would disperse and future designs could depend on outside suppliers. The EU–Ukraine alliance shows a real cooperation channel, not evidence of an impending cut.

    Uncertainty and timing: Hypothetical, 2027–2032. The trigger is a sharp, unplanned budget decision; signs would be cancelled research contracts and delayed joint trials.

    The impact would be smaller if several partners retained long-term funding.

    Possible trigger: A major grid failure or prolonged supply restriction could force a power-hungry shared laboratory to curtail operations.

    Uncertainty and timing: Hypothetical at any point to 2035. The trigger is an extraordinary prolonged outage, not normal high energy prices; resilience depends on backup power and nearby alternatives.

    A distributed network could move some work elsewhere but not all specialised experiments.
