Risks

Mechanisms that could harm European research and innovation. None is an inevitable outcome.

  1. Different funding programmes may leave the factory-building stage uncovered

    Evidence today: A German hydrogen-support announcement and a smaller EU clean-industry research call fund different purposes. Comparing their amounts as if they were interchangeable would mislead.

    How the pathway works: The real risk is a broken sequence: research proves a technology, but no compatible finance supports its first commercial plant.

    European significance, horizon and uncertainty: European inventions may be manufactured elsewhere if that gap persists. 2027–2033; the size of any shortfall needs project-level evidence.

    Counterforce: national finance and EU demonstration grants may combine successfully.

  2. New laboratories may open without stable careers for researchers

    Evidence today: One European initiative supports scientific facilities while a separate Marie Curie pilot offers longer research jobs. These budgets cover different activities and cannot be compared as totals for talent versus infrastructure.

    How the pathway works: Equipment produces little without technicians and research teams who can remain long enough to use it.

    European significance, horizon and uncertainty: 2027–2032: watch staff turnover and booked facility hours. The mismatch is a risk, not proof that a specific facility lacks workers.

    Counterforce: institutions can join equipment investment to long-term staffing plans.

  4. Computing dependence can turn an EU investment into an outside supplier's leverage

    Evidence today: Europe is expanding shared AI computing, but many leading processors, software tools and services are controlled by suppliers abroad.

    How the pathway works: If equipment upgrades, licences or support become unavailable, an EU-owned installation could still lose useful capacity.

    European significance, horizon and uncertainty: 2027–2035: the size of this risk depends on exact supplier contracts and available substitutes; no cutoff is assumed.

    Counterforce: multiple suppliers, open software and long-term maintenance reduce single points of failure.

    Outside-Europe comparison: a US study examines water risks around semiconductor production and irrigation. Water pressure is location-specific. Our EU inference is that European chip projects also need local water checks; the study does not measure European water risk.

Opportunities

Achievable actions that could improve European research and innovation, if their conditions are met.

  1. Use public AI computing contracts to require independent safety checks

    Evidence today: Europe has a public route to commission AI computing capacity, while research identifies gaps in who can inspect large AI systems.

    How the pathway works: Public buyers could require audit access, energy reporting and clear responsibility before buying computing time. This would use market leverage rather than assuming a new law solves implementation.

    European significance, horizon and uncertainty: 2027–2031: this connection is our proposal, not a confirmed EU programme. It succeeds only if contracts include usable tests and competing suppliers.

    Counterforce: overly restrictive contracts might exclude smaller providers.

    Outside-Europe comparison: a scholarly paper proposes African regulators share AI oversight knowledge. Its proposed clearinghouse is not an achieved result. We infer that European public buyers could test a related coordination model under EU law.
