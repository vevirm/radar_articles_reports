# Radar Criteria: R&I × Geopolitics + Foresight Methodology (EU-first)

## Date filter
- Published >= 2026-04-01 (verified publication date, not indexing date)
- Preprints allowed if dated in range; drop preprint when published version exists
- Strand C (weak signals): news items from the current scan window only

## EU relevance rule (applies to all strands)
Every item must have a clear EU angle: EU/member-state policy focus, EU-based R&I systems,
or direct implications for EU strategy (e.g., a US–China S&T paper qualifies only if it
draws consequences for Europe). Rank EU-focused items above items where the EU angle is
implicit or secondary.

## Topic scope

### Strand A — R&I under geopolitical change
Item must address BOTH research/innovation policy AND geopolitics. Match if it covers any of:
- EU technology sovereignty / open strategic autonomy in R&I
- research security and foreign-interference policy (EU toolkit, member-state regimes, trusted research)
- de-risking of S&T cooperation, incl. EU–China research relations
- export controls / dual-use rules affecting European research (EU dual-use regulation, national controls)
- fragmentation of global science as it affects European collaboration patterns
- EU positioning in US–China S&T competition; transatlantic R&I relations
- critical/emerging technologies policy with geopolitical framing (EU economic security strategy,
  critical technology assessments, STEP, chips/quantum/biotech/AI sovereignty debates)
- economic security measures affecting EU R&I funding, talent mobility, association agreements
  (e.g., Horizon Europe / FP10 international participation, third-country association)

Exclude: general geopolitics with no R&I angle; general innovation policy with no geopolitical
angle; single-country analyses outside Europe with no stated EU implications.

### Strand B — Foresight on these issues (methodology-first)
Match if the item is about HOW foresight is (or should be) done for R&I in geopolitically
uncertain contexts, with EU practice prioritised:
- design of foresight / horizon scanning / anticipatory governance for contested S&T domains
- foresight embedded in EU institutions (JRC, ESPAS, DG RTD strategic foresight cycle,
  member-state foresight units) — methods, evaluation, institutional design
- scenario methods for R&I policy under geopolitical uncertainty
- limits, biases, or evaluation of foresight in high-uncertainty environments
- integration of foresight with strategic intelligence, economic security, or risk assessment

Include foresight outputs (actual scenario reports) ONLY if they contain substantive
methodological reflection. Non-EU methodological work qualifies if transferable to EU practice.
Exclude: pure trend reports; foresight on unrelated topics.

### Strand C — Weak signals (news layer, anchored to Strands A/B)
Purpose: catch early empirical indications, from trusted news media, of developments that
publications already on the radar theorise, predict, or warn about.

Inclusion rules (ALL must hold):
1. Source is on the news whitelist below.
2. Item is factual reporting (event, decision, dataset, incident, funding move, policy step) —
   not opinion or analysis.
3. Item plausibly functions as a SIGNAL relative to at least one publication or recurring
   theme already caught in Strand A or B — it confirms, contradicts, accelerates, or
   instantiates a claim, scenario, or trend from that literature.
4. The connection can be stated explicitly (see "anchor" in output). No anchor, no inclusion.

Examples of the pattern (illustrative, not topical requirements):
- A Strand A report warns of research-security tightening → news: a member state suspends
  a university's joint lab with a third-country partner. SIGNAL (instantiates).
- A Strand B scenario set treats continued third-country association as baseline → news:
  an association negotiation stalls. SIGNAL (contradicts baseline).
- A Strand A paper predicts talent reshoring → news: measurable relocation scheme launched
  or notable uptake reported. SIGNAL (confirms/accelerates).

Exclude: news that is merely on-topic without connecting to a caught publication or theme;
routine coverage of known processes with no new development; commentary and editorials;
press releases reported verbatim.

News whitelist (extend by analogy):
- S&T policy press: Science|Business, Research Professional News, Table.Media (Research),
  Nature news, Science (news section), Times Higher Education
- General quality press (EU coverage): Financial Times, Politico Europe, The Economist,
  Reuters, Handelsblatt, Le Monde, NRC, El País — S&T/economic-security reporting only

## Source whitelist for Strands A/B (extend by analogy; tiers set ranking priority)

### Tier 1 — EU and European institutional
- European Commission: DG RTD, JRC (incl. EU Policy Lab / foresight unit), ESPAS
- EU advisory: Scientific Advice Mechanism, ESIR, ERC, ERA-related expert groups
- Parliamentary TA: STOA, TAB, Rathenau Instituut, POST and peers
- European think tanks: Bruegel, CEPS, MERICS, SWP, IFRI, EUISS, Clingendael, Chatham House
- European research institutes: Fraunhofer ISI, SPRU, MIoIR, TIK, CWTS, Nesta
- National academies and R&I councils of member states; OECD (STI)

### Tier 2 — Peer-reviewed journals
Research Policy; Science and Public Policy; Technological Forecasting & Social Change;
Futures; Foresight; Minerva; Technology in Society; Issues in Science and Technology

### Tier 3 — Non-EU sources (include only with explicit EU implications)
RAND, CSIS, Brookings, Carnegie, CSET, ASPI, NBER, SSRN, arXiv (policy-relevant)

## Quality gates for Strands A/B (must pass at least one)
- Peer-reviewed
- Published by a whitelisted or clearly comparable institution
- Authored by established S&T-policy or foresight researchers

## Hard exclusions (all strands)
- Op-eds, commentary, blog posts (Strands A/B); editorials and opinion pieces (Strand C)
- A/B items under ~2,000 words unless exceptionally substantive
- Consultancy marketing, advocacy without analysis, student theses

## Output per item

Strands A/B:
title | authors | source | date | link (DOI preferred) | type | strand (A/B/both) |
EU-relevance (direct/derived) | 3-sentence summary | 1-line relevance note

Strand C:
headline | source | date | link | anchor (which caught publication/theme it connects to) |
signal type (confirms / contradicts / accelerates / instantiates) | 2-sentence signal note
(what happened + why it matters for the anchored claim)

## Ranking & limits
- Strands A/B: max 15 items per scan; rank by EU-relevance (direct > derived),
  then source tier, then date desc
- Strand C: max 5 items per scan; rank by strength of connection to anchor, not by news prominence
- If <3 items in A or B, or 0 in C, report that explicitly — do not pad
