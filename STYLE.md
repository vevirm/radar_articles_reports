# Reader-writing contract

This file governs reader-facing text across Radar, Matrix, Trends, Risks & opportunities, Shocks, Read at least this, page introductions and link labels.

## The pair

Every evidence unit should answer two questions:

1. **WHAT** — what the source says, finds, changes or does.
2. **WHY** — the concrete consequence for EU research and innovation in geopolitical context.

A WHAT without a WHY is incomplete. A WHY that would work equally well on an unrelated site is generic and must be rewritten.

## Surface word budgets

- Matrix item: WHAT ≤ 12 words; WHY ≤ 15 words.
- Radar item: WHAT ≤ 20 words; WHY ≤ 20 words.
- Page introduction: ≤ 50 words and begin with a claim.
- Section lead-in: ≤ 25 words.
- Deeper explanatory/read-more material: 300–800 words where used.

Surface pages stay short. Technical depth belongs behind source detail, the Glossary and Stuff workbook/export.

## Radar

Use the source's real title. WHAT reports the source rather than evaluating it. WHY states the specific EU R&I geopolitical consequence: capability, access, control, dependency, collaboration, security, scaling, infrastructure, funding, standards or talent.

Use descriptive controls such as **Source summary**, **Open publication** and **Open source**. Avoid vague link labels.

## Matrix

Name both axes. Every placed finding is rendered as `WHAT — WHY` with the same grammar across the grid. Do not invent text to fill an empty cell. A genuine gap stays visible as a gap.

## Technical language

Keep standard policy vocabulary when it is necessary for accuracy, but do not make surface sentences denser merely to sound technical. Terms such as bibliographic coupling, citation burst, change-point detection, semantic shift, technology intelligence, dynamic topic model, weaponised interdependence and related method/classifier language should be defined in the Glossary and retained in Stuff/exports.

## Links and buttons

Anchor text describes what is on the other side. Do not use bare URLs or generic “click here”.

## Editing rules

Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs. Use short sentences with one idea each. Avoid throat-clearing, adjective stacks, rhetorical-question openers and filler sentences whose removal changes nothing.

## Release self-check

Before shipping a reader-facing change:

1. Check WHAT + WHY pairing where the page presents evidence claims.
2. Remove generic WHY sentences.
3. Count word budgets on Radar and Matrix output helpers.
4. Check Matrix grammar and axis labels.
5. Check link labels describe destinations.
6. Keep technical detail available in Glossary/Stuff rather than deleting it from the system.
