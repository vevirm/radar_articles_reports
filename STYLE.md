# Reader-writing contract

This file governs reader-facing text across Radar, Matrix, Trends, Risks & opportunities, Shocks, Read at least this, page introductions and link labels.

## The pair

Every evidence unit answers two questions:

1. **WHAT** — what the source says, finds, changes or does.
2. **WHY** — the concrete consequence for EU research and innovation in geopolitical context.

A WHAT without a WHY is incomplete. A WHY that would work equally well on an unrelated site is generic and must be rewritten.

## Surface word budgets

- Matrix item: WHAT ≤ 12 words; WHY ≤ 15 words.
- Radar item: WHAT ≤ 20 words; WHY ≤ 20 words.
- Page introduction: ≤ 50 words and begins with a claim.
- Section lead-in: ≤ 25 words.
- Deeper explanatory/read-more material: 300–800 words where used.

Surface pages stay short. Technical depth belongs in source detail, the Glossary and Stuff.

## Clarity rules

- Do not expose an unexplained specialist abbreviation when plain language fits. Expand **HPC** to **high-performance computing**, **MFF** to **EU long-term budget**, and similar terms on first surface use.
- Familiar institutional names may remain when the name itself is the subject, but the explanatory sentence must still be understandable without specialist knowledge.
- Do not end shortened surface text with `...` or `…`. A visible sentence should end as a complete readable statement.
- Do not recycle the same WHY across parent, branch and leaf items. Each WHY names the consequence specific to that unit.
- Prefer concrete subjects and verbs: who controls, who can access, what becomes dependent, what becomes more expensive, what collaboration changes.

## Radar

Use the source's real title. WHAT reports the source rather than evaluating it. WHY states the specific EU R&I geopolitical consequence: capability, access, control, dependency, collaboration, security, scaling, infrastructure, funding, standards or talent.

Use descriptive controls such as **Source summary**, **Open publication** and **Open source**. Avoid vague link labels.

## Matrix

Name both axes. Every placed finding is rendered as `WHAT — WHY` with parallel grammar across the grid. Do not invent text to fill an empty cell. A genuine gap stays visible as a gap.

## Read at least this

Every node has a label plus a short **WHAT** and **WHY**. Parent, branch and leaf nodes must explain different things. A source may support several nodes, but its generic relevance sentence must not be copied into all of them.

## Technical language

Keep technical vocabulary where it is necessary for accuracy, but do not make surface sentences dense merely to sound technical. Terms such as bibliographic coupling, citation burst, change-point detection, semantic shift, technology intelligence, dynamic topic model, graph anomaly detection and related method/classifier language belong primarily in the **Glossary** and **Stuff** workbooks.

## Links and buttons

Anchor text describes what is on the other side. Do not use bare URLs or generic “click here”.

## Editing rules

Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs. Use short sentences with one idea each. Avoid throat-clearing, adjective stacks, rhetorical-question openers and filler sentences whose removal changes nothing.

## Release self-check

Before shipping a reader-facing change:

1. Check WHAT + WHY pairing wherever the page presents an evidence claim.
2. Remove generic or repeated WHY sentences.
3. Count word budgets on Radar and Matrix output helpers.
4. Check Matrix grammar and axis labels.
5. Expand opaque abbreviations on surface pages or provide a clear definition.
6. Confirm no visible shortened sentence ends in `...` or `…`.
7. Check link labels describe destinations.
8. Keep technical detail available in Glossary/Stuff rather than deleting it from the system.
