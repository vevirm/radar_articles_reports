# Sovereignty-Frontier criteria — V17.6.1

The 4×4 Frontier is not a keyword taxonomy. Each occupied cell must have a supported R&I-system mechanism plus evidence for the cell's independence/competitiveness direction. Evidence-derived signals may use the underlying admitted title/summary and scanner-vetted EU scope; weak signals must carry the mechanism in the signal itself.

Strand B methods are excluded from Frontier evidence. The matrix is built from substantive Strand-A evidence, with Strand-C weak signals as contextual updates.

## Rows

- **Knowledge & people** — science, publication, collaboration, research security, researcher/scientist talent flows, careers, mobility and training.
- **Infrastructure & inputs** — compute, chips, data centres, energy, materials, facilities and other strategic inputs.
- **Conversion** — firms, scale-ups, production, capital, procurement and commercial/industrial capability.
- **Rules & institutions** — standards, regulation, export controls, funding, governance and decision capacity.

## Columns

- **A Opening** — more independent and more competitive.
- **B Costly autonomy** — more independent but less competitive.
- **C Productive dependence** — less independent but more competitive.
- **D Double loss** — less independent and less competitive.

## Knowledge & people semantics

The Knowledge row is explicitly reachable in all four directions:

- **Knowledge-A** — Europe/EU strengthens research capacity and autonomy through attraction/retention/return of researchers, brain gain, stronger European collaboration or comparable knowledge-capacity gains.
- **Knowledge-B** — research-security/screening/restriction measures increase protection or autonomy but impose collaboration, mobility, talent or research-performance costs.
- **Knowledge-C** — European research performance/capacity benefits from non-EU researchers, foreign expertise or external collaboration while remaining dependent on that external knowledge base.
- **Knowledge-D** — researcher/scientist outflow, brain drain, collaboration loss or talent loss reduces both European capacity/autonomy and competitiveness/performance.

Individual EU Member States and common adjectival forms (for example Ireland/Irish, Germany/German, France/French) count as European scope when the scanner has admitted the item as direct EU evidence.

A relevant radar item is not automatically a Frontier signal. Row relevance and directional evidence must both pass. Structural findings such as strategic dependencies, non-EU vendor or expertise reliance, supply-chain exposure, bottlenecks, capability gaps, foreign-rule reliance and EU capacity-building are valid evidence when the document establishes the relationship.

Brain Drain remains narrow: it requires actual European/EU-member researcher, scientist or research-talent outflow/loss. Generic migration, student mobility, geopolitical risk, economic coercion, regulation or research wording is insufficient by itself.
