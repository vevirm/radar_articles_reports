# Sovereignty-Frontier criteria — V17.8.2 balanced matrix

The 4×4 Sovereignty Frontier is **balanced by evidence opportunity, not by quota**. Each quadrant is searched and classified on the same scarcity basis. The matrix is allowed to be pessimistic or optimistic when the evidence warrants it, but the scanner must not structurally suppress one direction.

## Column logic

- **A Opening** — more independent and more competitive. A record may qualify through either a realised gain or a **concrete committed implementation step**: for example an awarded/committed investment, launched funded programme, approved project, facility build/deployment, signed capability partnership, procurement award, or adopted rule/standard with a direct R&I-capability mechanism.
- **B Costly autonomy** — more independent but less competitive.
- **C Productive dependence** — less independent but more competitive.
- **D Double loss** — less independent and less competitive.

Pure aspirations do **not** qualify as openings. A strategy, proposal, recommendation, roadmap, or statement that Europe *could/should/must* invest is insufficient without a concrete implementation action or observed result. Conversely, a launched and funded action does not need to wait years for final outcome data before it can be treated as an opening.

## Balanced discovery

Scarcity determines gap-search effort across all 16 cells. Empty A cells are searched alongside empty B/C/D cells; they are no longer postponed until risk cells are filled. There is no target share for openings and no forced symmetry in the final counts.

## Evidence discipline

Ambiguous evidence stays outside the matrix. Negative language in an anchor paper does not automatically veto a positive current development; the direct development controls the direction, while the anchor establishes strategic/EU relevance. External partnerships remain C rather than A when the gain depends materially on a non-EU actor without evidence of reduced reliance.
